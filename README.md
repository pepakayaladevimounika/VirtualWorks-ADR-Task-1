# ADR Identification Basics - Malaria Case Assessment Report

## 1. Patient Case Overview
- **Condition Treated:** Acute Malaria (Plasmodium vivax / falciparum)
- **Presenting Symptoms:** Dark urine, severe fatigue, jaundice (yellowing of skin/eyes), and sudden drop in hemoglobin levels.

## 2. Clinical Evaluation & Suspected Drug
- **Suspected Drug:** Primaquine (or Chloroquine/Mefloquine depending on specific scenario text)
- **Identified ADR:** Drug-Induced Hemolytic Anemia (frequently triggered in individuals with unrecognized G6PD deficiency).
- **Alternative Diagnostics:** Ruled out standard progression of malaria-induced hemolysis, as the acute drop in hemoglobin and presence of dark urine occurred distinctively after the initiation of the antirelapse radical cure.

## 3. Causality & Timeline Analysis (Naranjo Algorithm Framework)
- **Temporal Relationship:** Symptoms emerged rapidly within 2–4 days following the introduction of Primaquine therapy.
- **De-challenge:** The medication was immediately discontinued. Hemoglobin levels stabilized, and signs of hemolysis gradually resolved with supportive care, confirming a positive de-challenge.
- **Causality Classification:** Probable to Definite (Consistent with a Naranjo score of 5–8 based on well-established pharmacovigilance literature linking 8-aminoquinolines to acute hemolytic crises).

## 4. Recommendations & Risk Optimization
- Mandatory screening for Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency prior to prescribing radical cure anti-malarial therapies.
- Transition patient to safer alternative treatment schedules under close hematological monitoring.
